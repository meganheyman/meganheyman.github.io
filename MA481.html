MA 481: Mathematical Statistics

Prerequisites: [MA 382 (Introduction to Statistics)] OR [MA 381 (Introduction to Probability) + instructor consent]

Offering: In-person meetings occur on Monday, Tuesday, & Friday at 9am. Thursday's meeting is asynchronous, online.

Degree Requirements: This course counts toward
  • Statistics & OR Core for math majors
  • Statistics minor
  • Validation by Educational Experience (VEE) Mathematical Statistics credit for SOA, CAS,or CIA
  • Technical elective

This course introduces students to the analytical tools needed to establish the properties of common statistical methods. We will answer questions such as: "Why do sampling distributions of many statistics (beyond just the sample mean) become bell-shaped with large sample sizes?" "When is the sample mean the best estimator for the population mean?" and "What do we mean by "best" estimator?"

While building a proper foundation for inference, we emphasize the application of theory throughout the course. The course makes use of computing as a bridge between classical theory and the modern approaches used to characterize statistical methods.

This class will help prepare students to engage in statistical literature at an introductory level. Particularly, this class is aimed toward students considering graduate-level work in statistics. It does require a firm grasp of probability. Coursework in introductory statistics is also a requirement so that we can engage with content and eliminate the need for a lengthy review of the basics.

Learning Objectives

As in any statistics course, we emphasize statistical literacy and reasoning. This class also emphasizes the underlying theory of statistical methodology. Specifically, after taking this course, you will be able to:

  • Describe the role of probability theory in statistical inference for a population.
  • Compare and contrast modes of convergence and describe their role in sampling distributions.
  • Construct a proof to establish a property of an estimator or sequence of random variables.
  • Given an analysis situation, formulate research questions as measurable statements about parameter(s) in the population.
  • Associate a statistical method with various statistical properties such as consistency, unbiasedness, and asymptotic normality.
  • Clearly communicate statistical theory and its applications.
  • Compare and contrast mathematical and statistical thinking.
